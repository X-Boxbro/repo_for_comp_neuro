- neural decoding:从神经活动推断刺激或状态
    - scientific question:
        if an external stimulus is encoded by a neuron's spike response,can we infer which stimulus caused the observed response?
        this is the inverse problem of neural coding: encoding maps stimulus to neural response,while decoding maps neural response back to an estimate of the stimulus.
    - minimal example:
        suppose there are two possible stimuli,A and B,and we observe a spike response r.
        we can compare response features under A and B,such as spike count,mean firing rate,or spike timing.
        a decoder chooses the stimulus whose response distribution gives the larger probability for the observed response.
        this is only a toy decoder:the choice of response feature determines how much temporal information is preserved.
    - basic probability and Bayes' theorem:
        let S be the stimulus and R be the observed neural response.
        the joint probability satisfies P(S,R)=P(R|S)P(S).
        summing over possible stimuli gives P(R)=sum_s P(R|s)P(s).
        Bayes' theorem is P(S|R)=P(R|S)P(S)/P(R).
        P(S|R) is the posterior belief about the stimulus,P(R|S) is the likelihood,the prior P(S) describes belief before observing the response,and P(R) is the evidence used for normalization.
        for two equally likely stimuli,the decoder can often compare P(R|A) and P(R|B) directly;with unequal priors,the prior changes the decision threshold.
    - connection with computation:
        neural decoding can be implemented with a generative model of spike counts or spike times.
        a simple first step is to estimate response statistics from LIF trials,then use those statistics to classify A or B.
        accuracy,confusion matrix,and the effect of keeping or discarding spike timing are useful diagnostics.
- Fourier transform:把时间信号转换到频率表示
    - idea:
        a time signal x(t) can be represented as a weighted combination of sinusoidal components with different frequencies.
        the continuous Fourier transform is X(f)=integral x(t) exp(-i 2 pi f t) dt.
        the inverse transform reconstructs x(t)=integral X(f) exp(i 2 pi f t) df.
        in practice,a sampled signal is analyzed with the discrete Fourier transform(DFT),usually computed efficiently by the FFT.
    - interpretation:
        the magnitude |X(f)| describes how strongly a frequency is present,and the phase describes its relative timing.
        the power spectrum is proportional to |X(f)|^2.
        a short time window gives limited frequency resolution,while a long window gives better frequency resolution but less temporal localization.
    - connection with computational neuroscience:
        Fourier analysis can reveal oscillations in membrane potentials,local field potentials,and population activity.
        it can also describe the noise spectrum of spike trains and help compare a signal's rate information with its temporal structure.
        an ideal white-noise spectrum is flat over frequency,while biological filtering and network dynamics can emphasize or suppress particular frequencies.
- elementary stochastic processes:Bernoulli process and Poisson process
    - Bernoulli process:
        divide time into discrete steps. At each step,a Bernoulli random variable X_t takes value 1 with probability p and 0 with probability 1-p.
        assuming independent steps,the number of successes in n steps is N_n=sum_t X_t and follows a binomial distribution:
        P(N_n=k)=C(n,k)p^k(1-p)^(n-k).
        E[N_n]=np and Var[N_n]=np(1-p).
        in neural modeling,a Bernoulli process can represent whether a spike occurs in each small time bin.
        when the bin width is dt and p is approximately lambda*dt,the probability of two or more spikes in one sufficiently small bin is negligible.
    - Poisson process:
        a Poisson process N(t) counts events accumulated over continuous time.
        it has independent increments,and the number of events in an interval of length T follows
        P(N(T)=k)=exp(-lambda*T)(lambda*T)^k/k!.
        therefore E[N(T)]=lambda*T and Var[N(T)]=lambda*T.
        the waiting time between successive events follows an exponential distribution with density lambda*exp(-lambda*s).
        starting from independent Bernoulli bins with p=lambda*dt,letting dt approach zero while keeping lambda fixed gives the Poisson limit.
        the probability of one event in T is approximately (T/dt)(lambda*dt)=lambda*T at first order,and the full limit yields the Poisson distribution.
    - connection with spike trains:
        a homogeneous Poisson process is a simple model for irregular background spike arrivals.
        lambda is the mean firing rate;its coefficient of variation is 1 because exponential interspike intervals have equal mean and standard deviation.
        Poisson input can drive an LIF neuron,produce synaptic-current fluctuations,and provide a baseline for comparing more regular or more bursty spike trains.
